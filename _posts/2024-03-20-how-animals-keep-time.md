Learning bird calls came easily to me, and I credit that to years of ear training in classical music. Picking out an interval or a rhythm in a practice room is the same work as picking out one singer in a full canopy.

In classical music, rhythm is not a clock. The notes on the page have exact durations, which always exist, but only against the relative timing that a performer has chosen for each part of music. Chopin is said to have taught that the left hand must hold the pulse while the right hand phrases freely against it. The freedom only reads as freedom because a fixed reference sits underneath it. The time taken in one bar is measured against a pulse the listener can still feel.

A forest works in a similar way.

Anyone who has listened deeply outdoors knows that different groups of animals keep different kinds of time.

- **Insects are the percussion and the drone.** Crickets and katydids (the Orthoptera) lay down a pulse so steady that it stops sounding like rhythm and starts sounding like texture. Their tempo follows the temperature, and it has nothing to do with mood.
- **Mammals keep a beat.** A Red Squirrel scolding from a branch calls at regular intervals, over and over, like a metronome with an opinion.
- **Birds phrase.** A thrush sings a line, waits, and sings another, and the waits are never quite the same length. Birds are the right hand.

What strikes me is that none of these players is keeping time for the others, yet the forest seems to have a natural ebb-and-flow. The cricket is not accompanying the thrush. Each one follows its own reasons, and the music is what a listener hears when they overlap. In a concert hall, one performer bends time on purpose to tell a story. In a forest, the bending comes from many separate lives, and the story is whatever the listener can put together.

On stage, listening is intuitive. I hear small differences in my own playing as they happen, react to them, and build the narrative and drama out of them on the fly. Birding by ear asks for the same soft focus, which is what lets a distant, obscure, or uncommon song come through. In both cases I hold a steady pulse in one part of my attention and let another part follow whatever refuses to be steady.
