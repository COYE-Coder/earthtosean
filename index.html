Sean Carter

Remote Sensing Scientist · ML Engineer · Leidos Earth Intelligence

Currently based in the US, although I travel frequently to other countries

I’m a remote sensing scientist on the Earth Intelligence team at Leidos, where I work on various things. My direct work includes building remote sensing and geospatial machine learning pipelines for snow remote sensing in the Arctic. In addition, I’m involved with numerous capture efforts surrounding Geospatial Data Engineering, Database administration, and modernizing LLM-based systems.

I hold an MS in Systems Ecology from the University of Montana and a BA in Organismal Biology (with a piano minor) from Colorado College.

My work sits at the intersection of earth science, deep learning, and storytelling. With the geospatial work, I’ve worked on annual cropland mapping, fire detection using GEO-LEO imagery fusion, supply chain resilience modeling, and aquatic invasive species monitoring.

I lead a multifaceted life. I’m a data scientist, for one, but got there through the backdoor of field ecology (ornithology) and piano performance. I’m already on my third career! Lately, I’ve been working on my Vietnamese language (currently B2+), doing hot yoga, learning how to play jazz, and building an intentional co-housing project. This site is a place where I try to reconcile some of the facets of my life. Still searching for the perfect opportunity to integrate field biology, Vietnamese, machine learning, and musical performance.

my research

  1. Ecol. Ind.
    Annual cultivated extent and agricultural land use expansion across the central grasslands of North America, 1996–2021
    Sean Carter, Scott L. Morford, Jason D. Tack, and 5 more authors
    Ecological Indicators, 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF Code Website

    We developed a 30-meter resolution dataset capturing annual ’active’ and ’cumulative’ cropland (1996 to 2021) across the central grasslands of North America using an Attention U-Net convolutional neural network. Our bias-corrected estimates reveal that while active cropland showed no significant change, the cumulative cropland footprint expanded by 17% relative to the baseline (1996–2000), reaching 142.21 million ha by 2021. This divergence indicates substantial new conversion or recultivation of previously restored grasslands, occurring at a consistent rate of 0.98 million ha per year.

  2. Front. Big Data
    Testing a Generalizable Machine Learning Workflow for Aquatic Invasive Species on Rainbow Trout (\textitOncorhynchus mykiss) in Northwest Montana
    Sean Carter, Charles B. Rees, Brian K. Hand, and 3 more authors
    Frontiers in Big Data, 2021
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    We present a workflow that generates rapid spatial risk assessments on aquatic invasive species using occurrence data, spatially explicit environmental data, and an ensemble approach to species distribution modeling using five machine learning algorithms. We tested this workflow on Rainbow Trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) invasion in the upper Flathead River system in northwestern Montana. The ensemble modeling technique yielded high accuracy predictions relative to 30-fold cross-validated datasets (87% accuracy score).
